URGENT REQUEST FOR ACTION ON SIX (6) CRITICAL AMENITIES FOR GOWEST DISTRICT

I write to you today not as a politician, not as a party affiliate, but as a resident of Gowest District who is tired of watching basic dignity become a negotiation. For too long, our communities have been asked to be patient while the essentials of modern life s remain out of reach. This letter is a formal demand for action on six amenities that are not luxuries, not campaign promises, but the bare minimum any district assembly owes its people: a functional basic school, a properly equipped hospital, potable water, reliable electricity, affordable housing, and good roads . I am asking you to treat this as a matter of urgency,because the cost of delay is being paid in lost lives, stunted futures, and migration out of the district.

*1. A Functional Basic School Within Reasonable Distance for Every Child* The state of basic education in our district is a quiet emergency. Across the district, children walk 8-12 kilometers daily to reach the nearest school with a roof and more than two teachers. During the rainy season, that walk becomes impossible. The result is predictable: enrollment drops, dropout rates climb, and girls are pulled out first. Where schools do exist, they lack trained teachers, textbooks, latrines separated by gender, and classrooms that are not collapsing. This is not acceptable in 2026. A basic school is not a favor; it is the foundation of every other development goal you have. Without literacy and numeracy, we cannot produce skilled farmers, nurses, technicians, or entrepreneurs. I am demanding that your office immediately commission a district-wide audit of basic school infrastructure and teacher deployment, then publish a 24-month plan to ensure no child walks more than 3 kilometers to a school with at least six qualified teachers and basic sanitation. Education is the one investment that compounds. Every year you delay, we lose a cohort of children who will never catch up.

Snake bites, road accidents, and childhood malaria become death sentences because there is no ambulance, no oxygen, and no staff after 4 PM. The “clinic” we have is a single room with two nurses managing 5,000 people. A district hospital is non-negotiable. I am demanding the construction and full operationalization of a district hospital with a minimum of 50 beds, an emergency unit, a maternity ward, a functioning laboratory, and 24-hour staffing. Set a public timeline, and let civil society track it. Lives are not abstract figures in a budget line.

*3. Access to Potable Water for Every Household* We still rely on streams and unprotected boreholes that dry up or become contaminated for six months of the year. Waterborne diseases are the leading cause of outpatient attendance in our clinics. Women and children spend 3-4 hours daily fetching water, time that could be spent farming, studying, or resting. Potable water is a human right and a public health intervention. I am demanding a district-wide water scheme that connects every major community to a treated, piped system within 36 months. Publish the water coverage map for the district and show us where the gaps are.

*4. Reliable and Affordable Electricity for Productive Use* Half of our communities are still off the national grid, and those on it face daily outages that make refrigeration, welding, milling, and nighttime study impossible. Electricity is not about watching television. It is about productivity. Without power, agro-processing stalls, small businesses close at 6 PM, and students study by kerosene lamps that ruin their eyes and lungs.I am demanding that your office make rural electrification and reliability a top priority. Work with ECG and the Ministry of Energy to extend the grid to unserved communities, and where extension is not viable in the short term, facilitate mini-grid solar solutions for markets, schools, and health centers. Electricity is what turns a village into an economy.

5. Affordable Housing to Stop Rural Decay and Urban Migration* Our youth are leaving because there is nowhere decent to live and no incentive to stay. The housing stock in our district is aging, overcrowded, and built with materials that cannot withstand the weather. Young couples cannot afford to build, and there is no rental market. Affordable housing is not a luxury for district capitals. It is a tool to keep skilled people in the district. I am demanding that the assembly launch a district housing scheme using local materials and labor, targeting teachers, nurses, artisans, and young farmers. A district that cannot house its workers will never retain them. Housing anchors people. Anchored people build communities.

*6. Good, All-Season Roads to Connect Our Communities and Markets* None of the above can work if we are cut off for half the year. Our feeder roads collapse at the first heavy rain, making it impossible to transport farm produce, reach the hospital in an emergency, or get teachers and nurses to report to post. What takes 30 minutes in the dry season becomes a 3-hour ordeal in the wet season, and sometimes it’s impassable entirely. Good roads are the circulatory system of the district. Without them, schools stay empty, hospitals stay unused, and farmers lose 40-60% of their produce to spoilage before it reaches market. I am demanding a 24-month feeder road rehabilitation and maintenance plan that prioritizes routes linking farming communities to markets, schools, and health facilities. This must include proper drainage, periodic grading, and a transparent system for monitoring contractors. The assembly should also publish the road condition map and budget so we can track progress. You cannot talk about economic growth when produce rots on the farm because a truck cannot pass.

Hon. Chief Executive, these six demands are connected. Children cannot learn in a school without water, light, and a road to get there. A hospital cannot function without staff who have housing, power, and a way to reach patients. A farmer cannot sell produce if his children are sick and his village has no road to market. You cannot tell us to “think development” while we live without the basics.

I am giving you 60 days to respond publicly with a concrete action plan for each of these six areas, including timelines, budgets, and responsible officers. We will convene a community forum at the end of that period to review your response. This is not a threat. It is a statement that we are done being patient with avoidable problems.

Our district has land, people, and potential. What we lack is execution. Fix these six things, and you will have done more for Gowest District than any speech or festival could achieve. Fail to act, and history will record that you had the opportunity and chose inaction.

I await your favourable response
